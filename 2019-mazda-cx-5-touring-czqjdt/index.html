Your rights with an Ontario dealer

Ontario's Motor Vehicle Dealers Act (enforced by OMVIC) requires dealers to disclose certain facts in writing on the bill of sale. Telling you out loud doesn't count.

Buy it with a safety certificateWe only recommend buying this car with a Safety Standards Certificate, not as-is, and with an extended warranty. A certificate means the car met Ontario's minimum standard on the day it was inspected. It is not a warranty, and it is valid for 36 days. Our check found nothing that would stop it from passing.
90 days to cancelIf the dealer didn't disclose the distance driven, the make, model and year, past use as a daily rental, police, emergency, taxi or limo, a salvage / rebuilt / irreparable brand, or a total loss, you can cancel within 90 days.
All-in priceThe advertised price must include every fee except HST and licensing.
No cooling-off periodOnce you sign, the deal is done. Read the bill of sale and check the disclosures before you sign.
Check registrationEvery dealer and salesperson must be registered with OMVIC. You can look them up at omvic.ca.

What the readings mean

Very thin paint9 panels
Under 80 µm · Under 3.1 milsHeavy wet-sanding, aggressive polishing, or a factory defect (common on older Japanese and Korean cars). Do not compound.
Thin factory paint4 panels
80–100 µm · 3.1–3.9 milsTypical for modern economy cars (Honda, Toyota, Mazda, Subaru) with water-based paint. Polish with caution.
Standard factory paint
100–150 µm · 4.0–5.9 milsNormal for most North American and European vehicles. Safe for standard paint correction.
Thick factory paint
150–200 µm · 6.0–7.9 milsNormal for luxury brands (Porsche, Mercedes, BMW), or areas sprayed twice on the assembly line.
Repainted
200–350 µm · 8.0–13.8 milsFull panel respray, heavy primer, or a very thin skim coat of glazing putty.
